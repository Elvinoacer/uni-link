Architectural and Functional Engineering Framework for a Hyperlocal University Social EcosystemThe modernization of the collegiate experience is increasingly mediated through digital interfaces that facilitate social connection, resource exchange, and real-time community engagement. To design a social media platform tailored for a university environment—one that mirrors the functional efficiency of X (formerly Twitter) while incorporating niche campus utilities—requires a sophisticated understanding of distributed systems, community psychology, and hyperlocal logistics. This report delineates the exhaustive requirements for such a system, focusing on a decentralized access model that prioritizes community growth without immediate institutional integration or restrictive authentication barriers.Architectural Scaffolding and Distributed System DesignThe technical success of a social media platform rests upon its ability to handle high-concurrency environments characterized by a high read-to-write ratio. In a university setting, traffic patterns are often bursty, dictated by the academic calendar, campus crises, or major social events. Consequently, the underlying architecture must support horizontal scalability, low-latency reads, and high availability to remain robust during traffic spikes.1High-Level Microservices ArchitectureA monolithic architecture is unsuitable for a platform intended to serve hundreds of thousands of active users simultaneously. Instead, a decoupled microservices approach ensures that specific functional failures do not compromise the entire ecosystem.
